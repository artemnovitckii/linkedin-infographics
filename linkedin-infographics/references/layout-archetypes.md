# Layout archetypes

Show `assets/layout-wireframes.png` before writing final infographic copy. Recommend the strongest two options for the source, but let the user choose A, B, C, or D. Stop after presenting the options.

## A — Journey Rail

Use for ordered tutorials, workflows, installation guides, checklists, and processes where sequence matters. Animate a signal along a gutter rail; never route motion through content cards.

## B — Split Engine

Use for automation systems, transformations, before/after explanations, and input-to-output stories. Place raw inputs on the left, the mechanism in the center, and deliverables on the right. Animate material entering the engine and outputs becoming ready.

## C — Orbit Map

Use for frameworks, tool stacks, ecosystems, decision maps, comparisons, or one central principle with several related parts. Place the core concept in the center and distribute supporting nodes around it. Animate one clean pulse from the core to each node.

## D — Editorial Stack

Use for reports, research summaries, lessons, data-backed breakdowns, and posts with one dominant insight plus supporting evidence. Lead with a hero result, place supporting modules below it, and finish with a proof strip. Reveal in editorial reading order.

## Selection rules

- Choose structure from the content relationship, not visual novelty.
- Do not reuse the same archetype for consecutive posts when another structure fits equally well.
- Treat theme, palette, illustration style, and motion as separate from layout. Changing color alone does not create a new layout.
- Record the chosen archetype in the working content brief before producing final copy.
- If none fits, create a new low-fidelity wireframe and obtain approval before implementing it.
