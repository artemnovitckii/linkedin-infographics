# Signal-paper design system

The renderer uses a coded editorial-poster system designed for LinkedIn's feed.

## Format

- Canvas: 800 × 1000 pixels.
- Duration: 10 seconds.
- Master frame rate: 15 fps; optimized GIF: 10–12 fps.
- Target GIF size: 5 MB or less.
- Deliverables: looping GIF, PNG cover, editable JSON, caption, QA report.

## Visual grammar

- Warm paper background with a restrained 20-pixel engineering grid.
- Near-black typography with cobalt routing lines and hot-orange active states.
- Bricolage Grotesque for the promise; IBM Plex Sans and Mono for operational detail.
- Five illustrated cards follow a dedicated vertical motion rail. Completed steps remain visible; upcoming steps recede.
- Motion communicates state: hold on a step, travel through the gutter, then activate the next step. Never send the signal through a content card.
- When tool compatibility is part of the story, show the supported marks at equal visual weight in a dedicated lockup. Keep the infographic skill visually dominant and never imply endorsement.
- Use the conversion footer for resource-led posts: one large CTA, one named resource, and one compressed proof line. Do not repeat the CTA or compatibility claim elsewhere in the footer.
- When an author signature is present, integrate it into the footer proof rail with a local portrait, name, and one public plug. Keep it subordinate to the post CTA and do not imitate another creator's standalone signature badge.

## Quality bar

- Preserve one dominant headline and one unmistakable reading route.
- Keep all body copy readable at feed size.
- Use no stock illustration, gradient blob, glassmorphism, or generic dashboard cards.
- Inspect both the cover and at least three animation frames before publishing.
- Rewrite overflowing copy; never shrink body text below the template's set size.
